Northern Ireland (Belfast) British Pound (£) Days 7–8
Republic of Ireland (Dublin, Galway, Killarney) Euro (€) Days 8–17

Tipping Guide

Tipping is much more relaxed than in the US. Staff in both countries earn a fair minimum wage, so tips are appreciated but not expected the same way.

Situation What to do
Restaurants (sit-down) 10–15% if no service charge is already on the bill
Pubs / Bars Not expected. Round up if you want.
Taxis Round up to the nearest pound/euro
Hotels Not expected, but appreciated for exceptional help
Tour guides A few pounds/euros appreciated for good tours

Electrical Outlets

Both Scotland and Ireland use Type G plugs (three rectangular prongs). The voltage is 230V / 50Hz. You'll need a US-to-UK adapter — the same one works in both countries. Your phone and laptop chargers are dual-voltage, so you just need the adapter, not a converter. Buy one before you go!

Weather & What to Expect

July is the warmest month, but "warm" here means 55–66°F (12–19°C). Expect rain at some point almost every day — usually brief showers, not all-day downpours. The upside? July has the longest daylight hours, with sunset not until around 10 PM. That means extra time to explore.

Good to Know

Driving Side

They drive on the LEFT. Look right first when crossing the street! Many crosswalks have "LOOK RIGHT" painted on the ground to help.

Tap Water

Safe to drink everywhere in both countries. Bring a reusable bottle and save money.

WiFi & Data

Free WiFi is common in hotels, cafes, and restaurants. Check with your carrier about international plans or pick up a local SIM.

Safety: Both Scotland and Ireland are very safe countries for travelers. Normal precautions apply: keep an eye on your belongings in crowded tourist spots, stay aware of your surroundings, and stick together as a group. Emergency number is 999 (UK) or 112 (Ireland — though 999 also works).
