Food & Drink to Try

One of the best parts of traveling is eating your way through a new place. Here's what to look for:

Scottish Must-Eats Scotland

Haggis, Neeps & Tatties
Scotland's national dish: savory oats and spiced meat served with mashed turnips and potatoes. It sounds wild, but it tastes like a rich, warm, savory pudding. Try it at least once!
Fish & Chips (Fish Supper)
Battered haddock (not cod!) with chunky chips. Ask for salt and brown sauce in Edinburgh style.
Cullen Skink
Creamy smoked haddock and potato soup. Incredibly comforting, especially on a dreich (rainy) Highlands day.
Scottish Porridge
Hearty oats made with milk or cream. Scots traditionally add salt, not sugar. A perfect breakfast before a long day of exploring.
Cranachan
A dreamy dessert of whipped cream layered with toasted oats, fresh raspberries, honey, and a splash of whisky. Summer in a glass.
Irn-Bru
Scotland's "other national drink" — a bright orange, caffeinated ginger-flavored soft drink that outsells every major cola brand in Scotland. The taste is hard to describe: bubbly, sweet, citrusy, with a ginger kick. Scots swear by it as a hangover cure. You haven't truly visited Scotland until you've tried one.

Scottish Sweets & Snacks

Shortbread

Buttery, crumbly perfection. A Scottish tradition since Mary Queen of Scots. Buy a tin to take home.

Scottish Tablet

Like fudge's crunchier, sweeter, more intense cousin. Made from sugar, condensed milk, and butter — it crumbles then melts. You will buy more than you think you need, and it still won't be enough.

Tunnock's Tea Cakes

Chocolate-coated marshmallow on a biscuit base, wrapped in iconic red-and-silver foil. A Glasgow institution since 1956.

Tunnock's Caramel Wafers

Five layers of crispy wafer, caramel, and chocolate in a gold-and-red wrapper. Arguably even more addictive than the Tea Cakes. Grab both and decide for yourself.

Walkers Shortbread

The gold standard of Scottish shortbread, baked in the Highlands since 1898. Buttery, crumbly perfection. The tartan gift boxes make great souvenirs.

Edinburgh Rock

Not what you'd expect from the name — it's a soft, crumbly, pastel-colored candy stick that melts on your tongue. Made in Edinburgh since the 1800s. Nothing like regular rock candy.

Empire Biscuits

Two biscuits sandwiched with jam, topped with icing and a cherry. Found in every Scottish bakery.
