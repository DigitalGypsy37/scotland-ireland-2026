Phrases & Slang

Knowing a few local words will make locals smile (and make you sound cool). Here's your cheat sheet:

Scottish English & Scots Scotland

Aye Yes
Nae No / Not
Wee Small (e.g., "a wee bit")
Bairn / Wean BAIRN / WAYNE Child. "Bairn" in Edinburgh & east Scotland, "wean" (wee ane) in Glasgow & the west.
Bonnie Beautiful, pretty
Braw Fine, great, wonderful
Dreich DREEKH Dreary, wet, miserable weather (voted Scotland's most iconic word!)
Barry Great, awesome
Ken Know ("I dinnae ken" = I don't know)
Dinnae DIN-ay Don't
Cannae Can't
Lassie / Lad Girl / Boy
Och Exclamation of surprise (like "oh!")
Kirk Church
Stravaig struh-VAYG To wander or roam aimlessly. Basically what you'll be doing this entire trip.
Sassenach SASS-uh-nack An English person or outsider. Made famous by Outlander. Can be affectionate or not, depending on tone!
Auld Reekie OLD REE-kee Edinburgh's nickname, meaning "Old Smoky" — from the days when chimneys blanketed the city in smoke.
Gardy Loo! GAR-dee LOO Historic Edinburgh warning cry before emptying chamber pots out the window onto the street below. From French "gardez l'eau" (watch out for the water). You'll hear this on the ghost tour!
Scran Food. "Let's go get some scran" = let's go eat.
Scratcher Bed. "I'm off to my scratcher" = I'm going to bed.
Gob Mouth. "Shut yer gob" = be quiet. Also used in Ireland!

Scottish Gaelic & Whisky Words

Madainn mhath MAT-een vah Good morning
Feasgar math FES-ker mah Good evening
Tapadh leibh TAH-puh lev Thank you (formal)
Slàinte mhàth SLAHN-juh VAH "Good health!" — the Scottish Gaelic toast. Raise your glass and say it with feeling.
Lang may yer lum reek lang may yer LUM reek "Long may your chimney smoke" — a traditional Scottish blessing meaning "may you live long and well." A lovely thing to say to someone.
Uisge beatha OOSH-kuh BAH-huh "Water of life" — the Gaelic origin of the word "whisky." Yes, the English word whisky literally comes from this phrase.
A wee dram a wee DRAM A small measure of whisky. The classic way Scots offer a drink: "Fancy a wee dram?" The "wee" is non-negotiable, even if the pour is generous.
The Angel's Share The ~2% of whisky that evaporates from the barrel each year during aging. Poetically, it's the portion that goes up to heaven.
Fun language fact: Scottish Gaelic has no words for "yes" or "no." Instead, you reply using the verb from the question. So if someone asks "Are you?" the "yes" answer is essentially "I am."
