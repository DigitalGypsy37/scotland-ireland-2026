General Fun Facts

Location-specific facts are woven into the itinerary above. Here are some bigger-picture gems:

Scotland Scotland

The world's first color photograph was taken in Scotland in 1861 by physicist James Clerk Maxwell. The subject? A tartan ribbon. Of course it was.
The Picts were the original Scots. Before the Scots, Vikings, or English arrived, the Picts dominated most of Scotland from around 300–900 AD. They were fierce warriors famous for their blue body paint (their name may come from the Latin pictus meaning "painted") and intricate carved symbol stones that archaeologists still can't fully decode. They eventually merged with the Gaelic Scots to form the Kingdom of Alba — the foundation of modern Scotland.
The Angel's Share: When Scotch whisky ages in oak barrels, about 2% evaporates into the air each year. The Scots call this "the angel's share" — the portion that goes up to heaven. Over decades of aging, a significant amount vanishes. There's even a popular Scottish film by the same name. You'll smell it in the air near any distillery.

Ireland Ireland

Ireland has no native snakes. Legend credits St. Patrick with banishing them, but the real reason is that the island separated from mainland Europe before snakes could slither over.
Halloween was invented in Ireland. It originated from the ancient Celtic festival of Samhain, which marked the end of the harvest season and a time when the boundary between the living and dead was believed to thin.
Ireland is the only country in the world with a musical instrument as its national symbol — the harp. You'll see it everywhere, including on Euro coins minted in Ireland.
